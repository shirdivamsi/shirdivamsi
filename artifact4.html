Project Overview

This artifact documents a guided conversation with the Machine Learning Training Methods Coach, an AI chatbot designed to reinforce foundational concepts in how machine learning models are trained. Through interactive dialogue, I worked through seven core questions covering supervised learning, unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, the role of algorithms, the basic steps of model training, the importance of iteration, and the role of data. Rather than simply repeating definitions, I connected each concept to real-world scenarios drawn from my professional background as a Data Analyst.

Objective

The objective of this activity was to build a solid conceptual foundation in machine learning training methods before advancing into more technical coursework. Engaging in dialogue with an AI chatbot, rather than passively reading material, encouraged me to actively construct and articulate my own understanding of each concept.

Process

I accessed the Machine Learning Training Methods Coach through the SchoolAI space provided by my instructor and worked through the activity in the following steps:

  • Responded to the coach's follow-up prompts, which repeatedly asked me to explain the same idea a second way or apply it to a new example, pushing the answers from definitions toward applied reasoning.

Key Concepts Explored

Supervised Learning

A model learns from labeled data, comparing its predictions to known correct answers and adjusting its internal parameters to reduce error. I related this to validating a predictive model against historical outcomes, such as predicting late invoice payments using past invoice data where the actual outcome is already known.

Real-world example and issue resolved: In the invoice example, the specific issue is that "late payment" is only a reliable label if it is defined consistently, for instance if one team's data marks an invoice late after 5 days past due while another team uses a different grace period, the model will learn an inconsistent threshold and its predictions will not hold up across customer segments. The resolution is standardizing the label definition before training and then checking that model accuracy holds consistently across different customer groups, not just as a single aggregate score.

Unsupervised Learning

With no labeled "answer key," the model searches for structure or natural groupings within the data itself. I connected this to customer segmentation, where transactions are grouped by behavioral similarity without predefined categories, similar to exploratory data analysis.

Real-world example and issue resolved: The specific issue in customer segmentation is that clustering purely on transaction amount can group a customer who makes one large purchase a year with a customer who makes frequent small purchases, even though their actual buying behavior is very different. The resolution is incorporating behavioral features such as purchase frequency and product category alongside amount, then validating that the resulting segments actually differ in real behavior, like repeat purchase rate, before using them to make a business decision.

Reinforcement Learning

An agent learns through trial and error, taking actions and receiving rewards or penalties, then adjusting its strategy over many iterations to maximize cumulative reward. This approach is fundamentally different from labeled or exploratory data analysis, since it relies on a continuous feedback loop rather than a fixed dataset.

Real-world example and issue resolved: A relevant example is an automated approval workflow that learns which decisions reduce risk over time. The specific issue is that the agent can settle on a policy that performs reasonably well and stop testing alternatives, missing a better policy it never tried. The resolution is deliberately keeping some ongoing exploration built into the policy, rather than locking in permanently on the first strategy that produces acceptable results.

The Role of Algorithms, Training Steps, Iteration, and Data

Algorithms provide the rules that govern how a model updates itself based on feedback. Training a model generally follows a repeatable process: preparing data, splitting it into training and test sets, making predictions, measuring error, and adjusting accordingly. Repetition allows the model to make small, incremental corrections rather than solving the problem in a single pass, and the quality and variety of the training data ultimately determines how well the model generalizes to new situations.

Professional Reflection

I built this artifact with two audiences in mind: my instructor, who is evaluating whether I engaged critically with the concepts rather than restating them, and future portfolio readers, such as potential employers, who would want to see applied reasoning rather than textbook definitions. That is why each concept here is paired with a specific real-world example and a concrete issue it could cause, rather than a general definition alone.

The most useful feedback came from the coach's habit of asking me to explain the same idea a second time, in a different example. When I first explained reinforcement learning in fairly general terms, the coach's follow-up questions pushed me to ground it in a specific workflow instead, which is what shaped the approval-workflow example above. I revised my answers in the moment based on that push rather than keeping my first, more general version.

This activity reinforced how closely foundational machine learning concepts relate to the analytical work I already do as a Data Analyst. Explaining supervised learning through invoice prediction, or unsupervised learning through customer segmentation, helped me see that comparing predictions to known outcomes, iterating to reduce error, and relying on quality data are extensions of practices I already use in reporting and data validation. Reinforcement learning stood out as the most conceptually distinct approach, since it depends on a continuous feedback loop rather than a static, historical dataset.

Beyond the technical content, this exercise demonstrated the value of AI-assisted learning as a collaborative process. Rather than simply receiving answers, I was prompted to reason through each concept and express it in my own words, which deepened my understanding and prepared me to apply these training methods more confidently as I continue my coursework in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning.
